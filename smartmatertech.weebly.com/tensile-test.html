​Tensile Test Standard Procedure

  • Gauge Length: (Enter gauge length, usually in millimeters)
  • Quantity: (Enter the number of samples required for testing)
2. Testing Equipment
  • Tensile Testing Machine: (Enter model and specifications)
  • Length Gauge: (Enter model and resolution)
  • Testing Software: (Enter software name and version)
3. Preparation
  • Check the operational status of the tensile testing machine and ensure it meets standard requirements.
  • Install sample grips to securely hold the samples without slippage or distortion.
  • Set up the testing software according to the standard procedure and calibrate the length gauge.
4. Sample Preparation
  • Select the required number of samples from the material and label them appropriately for identification.
  • Ensure the sample surfaces are clean and free from any dirt or surface defects.
5. Testing Procedure
  1. Mount the sample onto the grips of the tensile testing machine, ensuring uniform loading between the grips.
  2. Set the testing machine to the specified tensile speed (Enter specific speed, usually in minutes or seconds).
  3. Set the strain rate to (Enter specific strain rate, usually in minutes or seconds).
  4. Initiate the test and record the applied tensile force and displacement data throughout the test.
  5. Continue the test until the sample fractures or reaches the designated endpoint.
  6. Upon test completion, stop the testing machine and record the fracture location.
  7. Remove the sample remnants from the grips and proceed with testing the next sample.
6. Data Analysis
  • Calculate the stress-strain curve based on the obtained tensile force and displacement data.
  • Analyze the tensile properties of the samples, including ultimate strength, yield strength, elongation at fracture, etc.
7. Results Reporting
  • Summarize the test data and perform statistical analysis.
  • Prepare a test report including sample information, testing methodology, data results, and conclusions.
8. Safety Precautions
  • Adhere to safety operating procedures during the testing process to prevent accidents.
  • Upon test completion, shut down the testing machine and clean the work area.

​Incremental Strain-Controlled Cycling Procedure
1. Equipment Setup
  • Tensile Testing Machine: Set up the machine with appropriate grips and ensure it is in proper working condition.
  • Strain Measurement Device: Utilize a strain gauge or extensometer to measure strain accurately.
  • Data Acquisition System: Employ a system to record applied load and corresponding strain data.
2. Initial Calibration
  • Calibrate the strain measurement device to ensure accurate strain readings throughout the test.
3. Cycling Procedure
  1. Initialization:
    • Start with the initial specimen.
    • Apply a preload to ensure proper contact between grips and specimen.
  2. Cycling Loop:
    • Begin loading the specimen at a low strain rate.
    • Incrementally increase the applied strain until reaching the maximum desired strain level.
    • Hold the specimen at the maximum strain for a predetermined duration to allow for stabilization and data collection.
    • Unload the specimen at a controlled rate until reaching a specified low strain level or stress value.
    • Hold the specimen at the low strain level to ensure unloading is complete.
    • Repeat the loading and unloading steps for a predetermined number of cycles or until failure.
  3. Data Collection:
    • Record applied load and corresponding strain values at regular intervals during each loading and unloading cycle.
    • Monitor any changes in mechanical behavior such as stress relaxation or cyclic hardening.
  4. Analysis:
    • Analyze the collected data to assess the material's cyclic response, including hysteresis loops, fatigue life, and fatigue strength.
4. Safety Precautions
  • Adhere to safety protocols throughout the testing procedure to prevent accidents or equipment damage.
  • Ensure that the testing machine is properly calibrated and maintained to ensure accurate and reliable results.
This cycling procedure provides a systematic approach to investigate the cyclic behavior of materials under incremental strain-controlled loading conditions. Adjustments may be made based on specific material properties and testing requirements.
