Protection
When working with UV light, particularly in processes like UV curing or cross-linking, there are several safety precautions to consider due to the harmful effects of UV radiation. Here are the main safety concerns and guidelines:
1. Eye Damage (Photokeratitis and Cataracts)
  • Risk: UV radiation can cause damage to the cornea, leading to conditions like photokeratitis, a painful inflammation similar to sunburn but on the eye. Long-term exposure can increase the risk of cataracts.
  • Prevention:
    • Always wear UV-blocking safety goggles or face shields with proper ratings (ANSI Z87.1 or equivalent).
    • Avoid looking directly at UV light sources.
2. Skin Burns
  • Risk: Prolonged exposure to UV radiation can result in skin burns, akin to sunburns, and long-term exposure may increase the risk of skin cancer.
  • Prevention:
    • Wear UV-blocking gloves and long-sleeved lab coats to cover exposed skin.
    • Use sunscreens with broad-spectrum (UVA/UVB) protection if skin might be exposed.
3. Ozone Generation
  • Risk: Some high-intensity UV systems (especially in the UVC range) can generate ozone, which can irritate the respiratory system.
  • Prevention:
    • Ensure proper ventilation in the work area.
    • Consider using a fume extractor or a UV system that limits ozone generation.
4. UV Exposure to Non-Targeted Areas
  • Risk: Reflected or scattered UV light can affect areas beyond the immediate exposure zone.
  • Prevention:
    • Use shields or enclosures to contain the UV light.
    • Ensure that the reflective surfaces near the UV source are minimized or treated to reduce reflection.
5. Handling UV-Curing Resins/Adhesives
  • Risk: Many UV-curable resins or adhesives can cause skin irritation or allergic reactions.
  • Prevention:
    • Always wear appropriate gloves (nitrile or another chemical-resistant type) when handling UV-curing materials.
    • Work in a well-ventilated area or under a fume hood to avoid inhaling vapors.
6. Fire Hazards
  • Risk: UV lamps, especially high-intensity ones, can generate heat, and certain chemicals used in UV processes may be flammable.
  • Prevention:
    • Keep flammable materials away from the UV light source.
    • Ensure that your UV light system is well-maintained and has appropriate cooling mechanisms to prevent overheating.
7. Electrical Safety
  • Risk: Many UV systems use high-voltage power supplies, which can pose electrical hazards.
  • Prevention:
    • Ensure the equipment is properly grounded and regularly inspected.
    • Turn off and unplug the system before performing maintenance.
By following these guidelines, you can minimize the risks associated with UV light exposure during your work.

​Heightmap to STL
by mfussi January 02, 2012
​Summary
Small (and dirty ;) ) script to convert grayscale heightmaps to stl models. Usable for landscapes or terrain prints.
Supported Image Formats: GIF, PNG (preferred), JPEG, BMP
​Instructions

Please follow this tutorial:
http://adv-geo-research.blogspot.de/2013/10/converting-dems-to-stl-files-for-3d.html (Special thanks to James Dietrich for writing such a good guide)
After you finished step 10 convert the image with heightmap2stl.
Usage:
java -jar heightmap2stl.jar 'path to imagefile' 'height of model' 'height of base'
Arguments:
'path': Path to the heightmap (png, jpegs,... )
'height of model': Z scale of the model. Height of the max elevation.
'height of base': Height of the base, the model stands on.
https://sourceforge.net/projects/heightmap2stl/?source=directory - Feel free to contribute.
